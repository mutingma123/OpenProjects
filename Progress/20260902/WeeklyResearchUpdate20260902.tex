\section{Embedding Retrieval Capacity}
\label{sec:retrieval}
\projectline{Embedding Retrieval Capacity for Decision Support}

\sumpara The context defines the retrieval interface and the reason it matters to optimization. \sumparaend
\noindent\textbf{Context.}
\fin Dense retrieval encodes each query and document as one fixed-length vector, then ranks by geometric similarity. \fine
\fin This project studies when that interface loses relevance patterns and whether the resulting errors damage optimization decisions. \fine

\updatehead{1}{Current objective}
\sumpara The objective transfers the representation result into the proposal's decision setting. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin Relate retrieval-capacity limits to feasibility loss and operational regret in a proposal-ready guarantee. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{2}{Progress since the last meeting}
\sumpara The progress distinguishes completed exposition from the open proposal theorem. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin \statusdone Built a 72-slide plain-language dissection of Weller et al.'s theorem, free-vector experiment, LIMIT benchmark, and appendices. \fine
  \item \fin \statusdone Separated proved capacity from empirical observations and design implications to avoid overstating evidence. \fine
  \item \fin \statusprogress Adapted the paper's family-specific bound to the NSF Operations Engineering proposal. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{3}{Key result or lesson}
\sumpara The key result states the lower bound and the empirical gap without conflating them. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin For \(n\) documents, returned-set size \(k\), robustness margin \(2\gamma\), and vector dimension \(d\), realizing every size-\(k\) answer requires \(d\geq \log\binom{n}{k}/\log(1+1/\gamma)\) \cite{weller2026}. \fine
  \item \fin LIMIT uses simple queries with overlapping relevant pairs and shows trained models use dimensions less effectively than optimized free vectors. \fine
  \item \fin The theorem is a capacity floor, not a recall prediction, so the decision-level consequence remains the open proposal contribution. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\section{Bi-objective Trajectory Revision C1}
\label{sec:biobj}
\projectline{Bi-objective Intersection Trajectory Revision C1}

\sumpara The context defines the decision problem and the defect addressed by the revision. \sumparaend
\noindent\textbf{Context.}
\fin At an automated intersection, the paper trades vehicle delay against modeled crossing collision cost. \fine
\fin Revision C1 corrects how repeated conflict-cell encounters for a vehicle pair become one probability and loss. \fine

\updatehead{1}{Current objective}
\sumpara The objective closes the mathematical repair and the remaining evidence chain. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin Close revision C1 with auditable pair risk, calibrated scale, practical controller comparisons, and reproducible evidence. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{2}{Progress since the last meeting}
\sumpara The progress reports the two diverged branches and their present closure status. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin \statusdone The \texttt{revision/c1} branch now uses one arrival-time error draw and the smallest shared-cell entry gap per vehicle pair. \fine
  \item \fin \statusdone Regenerated costs, the control menu, break-even results, figures, and claims after removing the retired average and count detour. \fine
  \item \fin \statusprogress The \texttt{main} audit finds only Step 1 of nine complete, so the revision branch remains open. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{3}{Key result or lesson}
\sumpara The key result gives the adopted estimand and its current evidence boundary. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin For vehicles \(q\) and \(q'\), \(\gamma_{\min}\) is the smallest entry-time gap, \(p\) its event probability, and \(\kappa_c\) the collision price. \fine
  \item \fin The schedule cost is \(C^{\mathrm{cross}}=\kappa_c\sum_{\{q,q'\}}p\!\left(\gamma_{\min}(q,q')\right)\), and linearity permits the sum without pair independence. \fine
  \item \fin The eight-vehicle case still favors coordination over the fallback but not extra speed; absolute costs require calibration. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\section{Stochastic Multiple Knapsack Manuscript}
\label{sec:knapsack}
\projectline{Stochastic Multiple Knapsack Manuscript}

\sumpara The context defines the model and the claims tested by the review. \sumparaend
\noindent\textbf{Context.}
\fin The manuscript assigns uncertain tasks in a two-stage multiple-knapsack model. \fine
\fin Its Benders method separates initial assignments from scenario subproblems, and the review checks the human-centered claim, integer decisions, and experiments against the code. \fine

\updatehead{1}{Current objective}
\sumpara The objective rebuilds the manuscript around a contribution supported by executable evidence. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin Rebuild the paper around a defensible contribution, feasible integer assignments, and executable stochastic, human-outcome, and algorithmic benchmarks. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{2}{Progress since the last meeting}
\sumpara The progress combines the full manuscript dissection with the four-paper benchmark review. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin \statusdone Produced a full dissection of the formulation, decomposition, results, ten advisor points, and revision order. \fine
  \item \fin \statusdone Reviewed two scheduling studies for fairness and uncertainty evidence and two knapsack studies for algorithm evidence \cite{gulmez2026,perreault2025,mancini2024,masmoudi2024}. \fine
  \item \fin \statusblocked The claim remains blocked because the feasibility proposition is false and reported assignments come from a continuous relaxation. \fine
\end{tightitems}

\updatehead{3}{Key result or lesson}
\sumpara The key result narrows the contribution and identifies the concrete technical blockers. \sumparaend
\begin{tightitems}
  \item \fin The defensible core is a time-discounted formulation plus a decomposition proof of concept for its continuous relaxation. \fine
  \item \fin An all-zero solution refutes the feasibility claim, and 12 of 21 assignment variables are fractional rather than feasible integer decisions. \fine
  \item \fin The closest literature turns the broad novelty claim into three tests: stochastic value, human-outcome value, and algorithmic value. \fine
\end{tightitems}
